# Product thesis

Supersedes §2.1–2.3 of the MVP spec. The engineering sections of that spec (§4–§13) stand except
where an ADR says otherwise.

---

## 1. The problem, restated

The original thesis was: *developers using agentic tools retain the ability to review code but lose
the ability to produce it from a blank page; the atrophied skills are recall and reasoning.*

That is half right, and it is the cheap half.

Recall decay is real but inexpensive. Not remembering an API's exact shape costs seconds, and the
tool that caused the decay also compensates for it. Nobody buys a product to fix that.

What actually decays, and what is actually expensive:

1. **You do not know your own system.** You did not write it, so you cannot hold it in working
   memory, so you cannot review the next diff properly, so you approve it. Review quality collapses
   quietly and nobody notices until an incident.
2. **Nobody on the team understands module X.** Not "we're weak at LINQ" — "if billing breaks at
   3am, four people are guessing."
3. **You are slow exactly when it matters** — when the model is confidently wrong and there is
   nobody left who can tell.

These are felt, expensive, and observable. Skill decay against an abstract competency graph is a
chart. The chart is not the pain.

## 2. The product

Practice generated from **your own repository**. Point it at a corpus; it reads commits, merged
pull requests, ADRs, and structure, and produces exercises about code you are on the hook for:

> This function was merged under your name three weeks ago. What happens when `items` is empty?

> Here is a diff from `PaymentReconciler`. One of these three changes introduces a race. Which?

> Explain why `OrderProjection` caches by tenant and not by user. _(ground truth: ADR-0012)_

Plus **Lenses** — versioned, externally-authored rulebooks and perspectives (community Claude Code
skills are the first implementation) that inspect the same corpus and emit candidates against a
named, citable standard.

## 3. Why this and not LeetCode

|  | LeetCode | Whetstone |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Content | Curated shared bank | Your corpus |
| Relevance | Generic, disconnected from work | The code you own |
| Grading | Deterministic judge | Rubric with a **named external standard** behind it |
| Scheduling | You pick | FSRS decides, from measured decay |
| Purpose | Pass an interview | Keep understanding the system you are responsible for |
| Team value | None | Bus factor per module |

LeetCode structurally cannot do this — it has no access to your repository. A bare LLM cannot either
— it has no history of what you have forgotten and no scheduler over it.

## 4. Three problems this solves that hand-authoring did not

**Cold start.** The original MVP required users to author their own exercises. That is the Anki
failure mode: everyone likes spaced repetition, almost nobody makes the cards. It also produced an
internal contradiction — Milestone C promised dogfooding "with real content" in week 20, while the
authoring UI was ticket 5.1 in week 25. Generation removes the contradiction; authoring becomes an
override.

**Moat.** A generic exercise bank is now a commodity — any model produces infinite "spot the bug"
snippets for free. Content a model can regenerate on demand is worth nothing. The tenant's corpus
plus a decay model over it cannot be regenerated by anyone.

**Rubric quality.** Hand-written rubrics made the grader's job the hardest possible version of
itself: free text scored against one person's opinion. Grading against a real diff, a real ADR, or
a named Lens rule gives the grader ground truth and gives the user a citation instead of a verdict.

## 5. The marketplace non-goal, resolved

The MVP spec ruled out an exercise marketplace because *"cross-tenant data flow is the riskiest
feature possible."* That reasoning still holds, and Lenses do not violate it:

> **The generator is shared. The generated content never leaves the tenant.**

An ecosystem with no cross-tenant data flow, no content moderation burden, and no marketplace to
build. §2.4's row becomes a design note rather than a refusal.

## 6. Delivery surface

A separate site visited each morning to practise loses to Slack, Jira, and the actual job, every
time. The wedge is the moment before merging code you did not write — real stakes, no new habit to
build. The web app is the history and the team view; it is not where the daily habit has to live.

MVP still ships the web practice loop (it is the thing that must exist for anything else to work),
but the delivery-surface question is open and is tracked as **OQ-8**.

## 7. What this costs

Sourcing is new scope. It is paid for out of authoring:

| Shrinks | Why |
| --- | --- |
| 5.1 Exercise authoring UI | Becomes a candidate review queue, not a full authoring surface |
| 5.2 Rubric editor | Rubric criteria derive from a candidate's `Rationale` |
| 5.4 Skill graph editor | Skills derive from corpus structure and Lens rules |
| 2.12 Seed data | A real repository, not a synthetic 50-skill graph |

## 8. The dual mandate

This project is both a product and a deliberate skill-retention exercise for its author (spec §17).
The two goals conflict less under this strategy than under the last one: the domain core — FSRS,
graph algorithms, tenancy, concurrency, security — stays hand-built and untouched by this change.
Sourcing adapters and generation plumbing sit squarely in the "assistance is fine" category.

Where they still conflict, **the learning mandate wins for anything on the §17 list, and the
product mandate wins everywhere else.** `docs/agents/ticket-authoring.md` encodes that split into
how every ticket is written.

## 9. The premise is still untested

The riskiest assumption is not RLS pooling or nonce CSP — those are solved problems. It is *"will
anyone answer these twice."* A throwaway probe (milestone **Spike — Premise probe**) runs in
parallel with Epic 1: a script over the author's own `git log` asking three questions a day, no app,
no scheduler, no database. Its output is a decision about which sources are worth building, taken
**before** `IExerciseSource` is committed to the domain.
